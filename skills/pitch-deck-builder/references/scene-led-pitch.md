# The scene-led pitch (a stronger story spine)

Learned from a 14-slide deck that a senior presenter built and delivered well. It keeps the
6-step structure and 30/40/30, but the whole deck hangs on ONE concrete scene instead of an
abstract analogy. Use it by default when the product fixes a moment the audience lives
through. Fall back to the analogy arc (`structure.md`) when no single scene fits.

## The 12 patterns

### Story
1. **One persona, one moment, one question.** Name a role the room recognises, a time and
   a real question: "Monday, 9:00. The CFO asks how much we still owe Supplier A."
   The kicker carries the time stamp, so the scene feels like a scene.
2. **Fail twice before the answer.** First failure: the obvious gap (AI can't reach the
   system). Second failure: the obvious fix also fails ("Connected is not the same as
   understood"). The second failure is where your difference lives; competitors stop at
   the first fix.
3. **Name today's workarounds as the competition.** One slide: "Today there are two ways
   around it. Both cost you." Each workaround gets 3 short costs. The status quo is the
   real rival, not another vendor.
4. **Derive the pillars from the failures.** 2-4 one-word pillars (Access / Meaning /
   Control), each with its question AND a back-reference line: "Where it broke: the
   THB 12.4M answer." The audience already saw why each pillar exists.
5. **Replay the scene at the end of the solution.** Same kicker, same question, now the
   right answer, plus a check per pillar. This slide is the proof and the benefit at once:
   "Same question. A different answer."
6. **The CTA asks them to bring their own scene.** "Bring one question your AI can't answer
   today." It closes the loop and makes the demo about their data.

### Persuasion
7. **Show the wrong number, not the word "risk".** Put two real-looking numbers side by
   side (AI answer vs finance report) with the 2-3 reasons they differ. Label it
   "Illustrative example". A concrete mismatch convinces executives faster than any statistic.
8. **Place third-party authority right under the scene it proves.** A short verbatim
   analyst quote with source and date, in a quiet callout box at the bottom of that slide,
   not on a separate stats slide.
9. **Win every seat at the table.** One "Who gains" slide: one column per buyer role
   (executives, IT, security, business). Each column has that role's fear in quotes, then
   3 checks. Then ask: "Bring all four to the first workshop."
10. **Make the next step finite and priced.** The pilot slide has 4 steps (Discover, Build,
    Use, Decide), a scope limit (1-2 systems, read-only), a sourced cost estimate, and
    "A clear finish line. You decide what comes next."

### Honesty and craft
11. **Say the limits on the slide.** "Read-only in this release." "The record keeps request
    details, not your data." A stated limit raises trust and blocks the objection early.
12. **Gloss jargon inline, once.** Introduce a technical term inside the story in one
    clause ("through MCP, the standard way AI now uses company systems") instead of giving
    it its own slide. Product names appear only after the problem is felt.

## The slide frame (every content slide)
| Zone | Content |
|---|---|
| Kicker (small caps, accent) | The pillar or scene label: ACCESS, MONDAY 9:00, WHO GAINS |
| Title (one sentence) | The claim of the slide |
| Body | One visual: chat window, two columns, side-by-side numbers, flow, cards |
| **Takeaway bar** (full-width tinted band near the bottom) | The "so what" in one sentence: "One is slow. The other is unsafe. Neither scales." |
| Footer | Page number, small logo |

The takeaway bar is the line the presenter says last before moving on. Read the bars alone,
in order: they should tell the whole story.

## Visual restraint
- Light background, one primary colour, one secondary accent, one font family (plus a mono
  font only for code-like samples).
- Pillar colours become tags: the same chip (Access / Meaning / Control) appears under the
  architecture component that delivers it, on the replay slide, and on the CTA.
- Fixed grid: the same margins, kicker, title and takeaway-bar positions on every slide.
- Screenshot placeholders ("[ Screenshot: Playground ]") are fine in a draft; they are open items.

## Speaker notes in this style
Full spoken prose, written to be read aloud: numbers in words ("twelve point four million
baht"), one room question per problem slide ("Which one does your company use today?
Usually both."), and the last sentence repeats the takeaway bar.
